# Runner journal external freshness anchor: design investigation

**Disposition: design only; not installed, implemented, or approved for deployment.** This narrows the open anchor requirement in [dispatch/close investigation](runner-arbitration-supervisor-investigation.md) and [service boundary](runner-service-boundary.md). It does not make either existing prototype safe to integrate. No external authority, physical rollback-resistant device, privileged recovery mechanism, or protected Linux runner exists here. A second file, signature, MAC, SQLite FULL/WAL, separate UID, or hash chain on the journal's rollbackable disk does not detect a coordinated restore of that disk. Without a separately protected, non-rollbackable authority with the semantics below, freshness and even prior existence cannot be established: keep admission disabled.

## Concrete candidate and trust boundary

An independently administered **external linearizable register** stores one durable record per permanently assigned runner identity, outside the journal disk's rollback and administration domain. This is an abstract candidate, not a claim that a selected product provides it. Its authenticated API has linearizable read and compare-and-swap (CAS), durable acknowledgements, non-reusable identities, and an authenticated administrative provisioning/recovery channel distinct from the service's restricted update credential. Service updates can advance only the exact current record to a successor; they cannot delete, reset, move backwards, change identity, or provision a new identity. The authority enforces those restrictions rather than trusting a client. Reads must be fresh, authenticated and bound to the intended authority and runner, not a cache or replayable signed response. The authority durably retains identity allocation and recovery audit across its own failures and backups; restoring the authority itself to an old state violates the assumption. Authority unavailability, unknown outcome or equivocation closes admission. Authenticate and authorize every operation, protect credentials from the application and jobs, and prevent an old service instance from using them after recovery.

A real external quorum or hardware-backed monotonic facility might supply these properties, but neither is selected or verified. [OCI Object Storage's Python API](https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/tools/python/latest/api/object_storage/client/oci.object_storage.ObjectStorageClient.html#oci.object_storage.ObjectStorageClient.put_object) documents conditional `PutObject` using `if_match` and create-only `if_none_match='*'`; it also documents a default retry strategy for that operation. Conditional replacement alone is not a monotonic register, does not protect against an authorized older write or coordinated authority rollback, and does not prove this candidate's complete semantics. Any future adapter must explicitly control SDK retries and independently review ambiguous responses and its IAM boundary; no OCI resource or integration has been selected or provisioned. A TPM monotonic counter alone is not this register: a reviewed binding of counter, journal digest, identity and reset/ownership lifecycle is also needed. A register on another partition, disk snapshot, or host under the same rollback/administration control is not independent merely because it has a different path or UID. Host root/kernel can replace the verifier, steal credentials, falsify local journal observations or invoke runtime outside the protocol; this candidate does not defend against them. Separate administration protects against the app principal and certain storage restores only if actual access control and operations are independently verified. Hash collision resistance and correct authority, journal, storage and transport implementations are assumptions, not results of this document.

The record is `(version, runner-id, epoch, sequence, state-digest)`. The digest commits to a domain-separated, versioned canonical encoding of the complete logical journal state, including runner identity, epoch, sequence, every permanent request/tombstone, dispatch/admission decision, unresolved barrier and all state influencing future authorization. The journal must validate its schema, constraints, complete history and digest from a consistent SQLite snapshot, including committed WAL content; hashing only the main database file is insufficient. Its exact encoding, bounds and migration rules require a separate implementation specification. A digest proves equality with what the authority accepted, not the correctness of an operation, runtime termination, or that all authorization-relevant state was included.

## First provisioning: a distinct ceremony

1. The owner disables and fences all runner instances and runtime dispatch paths, selects a new never-reused runner identity, and verifies the independent authority's authenticated administrative record says **unallocated**, not merely missing/unreachable. Authority allocation history must make a deleted or rolled-back record distinguishable from an unused identity. An empty local directory is never evidence of first provisioning. Existing/missing/ambiguous authority state or a local collision stops the ceremony; never turn a failed reopen into provisioning.
2. Create an exclusively new journal with random journal identity, epoch and initial sequence, with the full initial state and identity included in the digest. Commit and durably flush it and its creation metadata using reviewed SQLite/filesystem ordering, then read it back and verify. Provisioning and administration are not exposed to the app or ordinary runner API. A failure leaves admission disabled and preserves artifacts for the owner.
3. The owner atomically allocates the runner identity in the authority with the exact initial record, durably records the ceremony in the authority's independent audit, and reads it back through a fresh authenticated read. Only after both exact records verify and the owner explicitly enables the one fenced instance can normal admission begin. A crash before allocation leaves an unallocated identity and a stranded local journal; it does **not** permit an ordinary service to allocate. A crash after allocation but before confirmation requires owner inspection and a fresh comparison, not a second allocation. If either side cannot be proved, remain disabled. Reusing an identity or allocating a replacement to evade a lost history is forbidden.

The runner identity and expected authority configuration must themselves be protected from substitution by the application and bound to the owner-controlled service configuration. The authority's authenticated response must distinguish unallocated from unavailable, malformed, unauthorized and allocated-but-missing; in particular a generic 404 is not sufficient proof of never having existed.

## Journal-first update and admission rule

Only one fenced writer may perform journal transitions or invoke the runtime. It holds a host-level exclusive writer/continuation fence for the whole protocol and runtime dispatch decision, with an independently reviewed way to ensure a predecessor cannot resume after replacement. A SQLite lock alone ends at commit and does not fence an old process holding an execution continuation. Do not issue reusable client dispatch tokens. Every decision that can affect admission, including close tombstones and unresolved barriers, is serialized through this protocol; no alternate runtime path may bypass it.

For a transition from exact current `A=(id, epoch, n, digest-n)` to `B=(id, epoch, n+1, digest-next)`:

1. With admission closed to competing operations and no external effect authorized by this transition, fresh-read the authority and verify exact equality with a validated journal snapshot at `A`. Missing, stale, invalid or inaccessible state stops all dispatch. Stage and commit the complete next journal state `B` in one SQLite transaction with durable commit; verify errors and durability assumptions, including WAL and storage flush behavior.
2. CAS the independent authority from exactly `A` to exactly `B`. A successful response means the authority durably committed `B`. If the response is lost or any error occurs, do not dispatch, do not retry an effect, and latch a stop. A fresh authority read may characterize the outcome, but does not silently resume this operation.
3. Only after a verified successful CAS may the live, unique continuation report a durable close or invoke a winning dispatch. Even then, a committed dispatch decision is only **dispatch-possible**: crash, timeout or loss after either commitment cannot establish whether invocation occurred. Reopening is inspection-only for that decision; it must never recreate the continuation. Keep all unresolved barriers. Close does not prove termination.

The protocol deliberately sacrifices availability. It requires all successors, including a close, to anchor before acknowledging success. A crash after both writes but before runtime invocation is indistinguishable from a crash after invocation unless further independently protected evidence proves otherwise; equality at restart is not permission to replay. Independently specify linearization with the supervisor, policy and budget: this anchor protocol does not combine their current separate databases or solve policy revocation races. Dispatch remains disabled until one authority covers those decisions and the continuation fence is established.

## Failure matrix: observation is not authorization

| Authenticated authority | Valid journal | Interpretation and action |
|---|---|---|
| `A` | `A` | Matching evidence only. Open inspection if the predecessor is fenced; respect all retained barriers and never replay a previous dispatch. New admission still needs all other service prerequisites. |
| `A` | `B` | Journal-first crash or failed CAS, or tampering. Stop; never automatically roll the journal back, advance the anchor, or issue a dispatch. Preserve both for owner recovery. |
| `B` | `B` | Both writes happened; crash may have occurred before or after an effect. Stop automatic replay; retained dispatch-possible remains uncertain. |
| `B` | `A` | Journal rollback/loss or contradictory observation. Stop and preserve evidence; do not recreate a missing journal or lower the authority. |
| anything | missing, corrupt, different identity/epoch/digest, impossible sequence, or unverifiable snapshot | Stop. A schema-valid older journal and coordinated restore of all local files are detected only because the independent authority still holds the newer `B`. |
| missing, unavailable, stale, invalid, unauthorized or ambiguous | anything | Stop; no inference of first provisioning or safety. |

An attacker able to restore both local disk and authority to `A`, or to substitute a different authority/runner identity, can make an old pair match. The protocol does not detect that attack. Also, if a journal write or authority CAS reports success before data is actually durable, power loss can violate the ordering; process-kill tests cannot establish power-loss behavior. The service must fail closed on read/write/fsync/transaction errors and on inability to validate these assumptions.

## Explicit owner recovery

Recovery is an out-of-band, audited administrative procedure, not an API accessible to Pilot or an automatic reconciliation rule. First disable admission, stop automatic restarts, fence/terminate all old service instances and continuations, and independently verify the runtime's exact workload/cgroup identities, termination and absence of restart paths where possible. Preserve the authority's current record and audit, journal including WAL, logs and runtime evidence before changing anything. Evidence about local termination cannot undo external effects. If any relevant identity, historical decision or termination is unprovable, retain global uncertainty and stop indefinitely; rebuilding the host alone does not make an old dispatch safe.

If a complete journal exactly matching the authority's current record can be independently recovered and validated, the owner may explicitly authorize reopening that exact state after the predecessor is fenced; all existing tombstones and unresolved barriers remain. If the journal is ahead of the authority or otherwise mismatched, there is no generic safe automatic repair: the owner must establish from retained independent evidence the complete set of historical decisions and the effect uncertainty, then use a separately reviewed administrative transaction that records the evidence, old and new records, actor and reason in the independent durable audit and advances to a new epoch without deleting tombstones or downgrading any possible dispatch. It must fence old credentials/instances before activation, bind the new journal digest in the authority, and apply the same mismatch-on-crash rule throughout. If complete history cannot be established, or the authority is lost/rolled back, no protocol here authorizes a fresh empty journal, replacement identity, barrier clearing, or resumed dispatch. This describes requirements for recovery, not an implemented recovery operation or permission to infer facts from a local backup.

## Required validation before implementation or deployment

A future disposable model test should enumerate both durable-write crash points, a lost CAS response, duplicate/changed requests, delayed close and dispatch, separate-process writer fencing, old continuations, first-provision interruption and privileged recovery interruption. Test both local-only rollback and coordinated rollback: the former must close against an unchanged authority; the latter is an explicit counterexample when the fake authority is rolled back too, not a passing security claim. Inject unavailable, stale, malformed, unauthorized, wrong-identity and equivocal authority replies, invalid journal/WAL and fsync errors; no failure may dispatch or provision. Check owner recovery preserves tombstones and uncertainty and cannot turn missing evidence into a new identity. A test double only characterizes the protocol under its assumed semantics; it cannot prove an actual authority has them.

Before any real use, select and independently review an authority and its durable linearizability, identity lifecycle, authenticated fresh reads, administrative separation, backup/restore and anti-rollback, credential rotation, resource exhaustion and recovery audit. Independently review exact Linux service/ACL/mount/UID and runtime fencing, and verify storage durability under actual power-loss conditions. No live service, host configuration, network authority, or production state was changed or tested in this investigation.
